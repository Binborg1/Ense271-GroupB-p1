## Project background

AIDS Programs South Saskatchewan (APSS) is a charity in Regina that has been running since 1985. It works with people living with or at risk of HIV and hepatitis C, and people at risk of overdose. Its services include the Queen City Needle Exchange, harm reduction supplies, HIV and STI testing, naloxone training and support groups.

APSS also runs reportneedles.ca. Anyone can use it to report a discarded needle by dropping a pin on a map, without giving their name. APSS staff then go and pick the needle up.

APSS has worked with University of Regina researchers on two projects connected to reportneedles.ca:

- **Mapping Needles, Reducing Harm** (completed, 2023 to 2024) used the needle reports to map hot spots, so naloxone training and peer support could go where they were needed most.
- **3 Infections, 1 Fight** (current, 2025 to 2027) uses those hot spot maps to decide where to set up pop-up testing for HIV, syphilis and hepatitis C.

These projects have produced journal articles, a community report, posters and presentations.

## Business need and opportunity

Right now there is no single place to find APSS research. The main APSS website has one short research page under "About Us", with no publications, posters or updates. Someone who hears about the work has to look on other websites to find it.

APSS asked for a separate research website where visitors can:

- browse current and completed research projects
- read journal publications and articles
- view research posters
- follow research updates through a blog
- move easily between the research site, the main APSS site and reportneedles.ca

While looking at the main APSS site, we also found problems that make it harder for people to get help:

- The homepage banner uses AI-generated art.
- Some icons look clickable but do not lead anywhere.
- Needle exchange and support services are hard to find from the homepage.

The project brief says the main site is not the priority. The research website is our main product (MVP). We are also proposing a redesign of the main site that fixes the problems above.

## Customers

### North Star customer

People affected by HIV/AIDS, hepatitis C and other infectious diseases. This includes people who use harm reduction services, the needle exchange and support groups.

These people mostly use the main APSS site, but the research site still has to work for them. Research findings need short plain-language summaries, and every page needs a clear way back to APSS services.

### Carryover customers

- Members of the public who want to donate, volunteer or support APSS
- People learning about these diseases, their history and the people affected
- People who want to report a needle or get one cleaned up
- Researchers, students and community partners who want to read APSS research

## Assumptions

- Both sites will be built in WordPress.
- The research site will be its own website, linked to the main APSS site and reportneedles.ca.
- The research content (projects, articles, posters and presentations) is already public, so we can use it to fill the site.
- We will plan with paper sketches and Figma, and keep our user story map in StoriesOnBoard.

## Constraints

- **Time.** The project has four activities with fixed deadlines. The final product is presented on December 2, 2026.
- **Skills.** Some of us are new to WordPress and GitHub and are learning them as we go.
- **No access to the live APSS website.** Our main site redesign will be a separate WordPress version that APSS could choose to use later.
- **Client priority.** The research site comes first. Work on the main site redesign cannot hold it up.
- **Sensitive audience.** Our users include people living with HIV and people who use drugs. Wording and images must be respectful and must not add to stigma.
